Refill

An empty chair is money walking out of the salon. Refill gives a cancelled slot to the next waiting customer who fits it, only messages people who said yes, and never books two people into one chair.

Try the product Request a product change

Try it in two minutes

  1. Open the product and choose "Try the demo" if it is not already marked Demo. Choose "Front desk" to open the invented Kesar Salon day.
  2. Open a future booked appointment, choose "Record a cancellation", then confirm with "Yes, [client name] cancelled".
  3. Open the amber gap. Review who fits and expand "Not offered, and why" for skipped clients. Tick the people to contact.
  4. Use the Send offers button, which includes the selected count (for example, "Send 3 offers"). Open a pending offer here or scan its phone QR code.
  5. Choose "Book" with the offered time. Return to "Front desk" to see the booking, then choose "Owner" → "Overview" to see the recovered value.
  6. Choose "Proof" from All views, or "Proof for judges" from the view menu, to open the Lab and try the case checks.

To check consent, use "Stop messages from Kesar Salon" on a pending offer before booking it. To check simultaneous booking, keep two offers pending and choose "Two phones, one slot" → "Tap both at once", or scan one code on each phone.

On a phone, the app can be installed: browser menu → "Install app" or "Add to Home Screen".

What the team inherited (version 1)

Historical reference for the business team's before-and-after comparison. The walkthrough above follows the current interface; the version 1 limitations below describe the original handover.

For the owner

The desk

Today’s appointments per stylist. Mark a cancellation; the app says how much notice was given (under two hours is "late"). It lists who fits the empty slot, who does not and why, and sends offers only after the owner approves.

For the customer

The offer page

A short message on the phone: the slot, the stylist, the service and price. Book, Not today, or Stop messages. If someone else booked a moment earlier, it says so and the customer keeps their place in the queue.

Trust

Consent and evidence

Every customer’s consent is yes, no or "not asked". "Not asked" is never treated as yes. Every action is logged with a reason and can be exported for the owner, or for an auditor.

The original gap to negotiate

The case card says your change is: "add service-duration and staff constraints before outreach." In version 1 the app knew how long each slot was and what kind of service it was booked for (hair, nails, skin). It did not yet know which stylist was actually in that day, or that a stylist who did hair could also do a facial. At that handover:

  • if a stylist called in sick, version 1 could not tell which of their slots to stop offering;
  • a freed hair slot with a stylist who also did skin could not go to a waiting facial customer, so a possible booking was missed.

Compare that starting point with the current "Owner" → "Team", "Staff" and "Proof" views. Record what changed and why it matters to a real owner, then request any remaining improvement through a GitHub issue. Engineering will reply with scope, an offer or a counter-offer.

The market, in numbers you can defend

All figures below are secondary sources or assumptions. Quote a number only with its source, or mark it "unverified".

How often chairs go empty

  • A 20% salon no-show rate is widely repeated, but its original source is hard to trace. Treat it as unverified.
  • A Booksy survey of U.S. salons found only about 2% of clients cancel without any warning.
  • Treatwell (UK) reports 2–3% no-shows per treatment, and says salons requiring prepayment halved theirs.
  • Most valuable for us: cancellations with some notice, because those slots can still be refilled. Ask owners how many they get per week.

So the pitch cannot be "we have a waitlist". Several do. Ours: the app checks consent, length and staff before any message, shows why each person was skipped, never double-books, and costs a flat fee per location rather than per seat.

Consent is a selling point

Meta requires opt-in before business-initiated WhatsApp marketing messages. India's DPDP Act expects consent that is explicit, recorded and easy to withdraw. Refill records who said yes, when and how, and honours STOP at once. For an owner, that is less risk of a blocked WhatsApp number or a complaint.

The growth experiment on the card

"Test opt-in waitlist messages with five consenting role players. Record who was approached, the response and what would count as a useful result."

  1. Before you start, write down what counts as a useful result (for example: at least 3 of 5 book within two minutes) and what would change your mind.
  2. Five teammates act as customers (label it as role play). Each gets an offer on their phone from the live app.
  3. Record: who, time sent, what they saw, what they did, how long they took.
  4. Include one who says STOP and one who is too slow, so you see the full journey.
  5. Chirag from engineering logs each session in docs/ROLEPLAY_LOG.md; you write the conclusions.

How to request a change (no coding)

  1. Make a free GitHub account if you do not have one.
  2. Open github.com/sirgreaseball/refill → Issues → New issue and pick MBA product request.
  3. Fill in: the problem, your evidence, the change (must-have vs nice-to-have), how we will verify it, the business reason.
  4. Engineering replies on the issue with scope, an offer or a counter. Agree there; that thread is your evidence.
  5. Log each step in Negotiation_and_Transfer_Log.csv.
